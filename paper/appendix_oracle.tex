\section{Oracle Verification of the Pressure Identity}
\label{sec:appendix-oracle}

The pressure identity in \cref{eq:pressure-identity} is exact for the linear-interpolant marginal
field. We verified it numerically on a Gaussian-mixture oracle where $p(x,t)$, $v(x,t)$,
$\Sigma(x,t)$, and the score $\nabla_x \log p(x,t)$ are available in closed form. The verification
compares two independently computed quantities:
\begin{align}
  a_{\mathrm{mat}}(x,t)
  &=
  \partial_t v(x,t) + \nabla_x v(x,t)\,v(x,t),
  \label{eq:oracle-material} \\
  a_{\mathrm{press}}(x,t)
  &=
  -\nabla_x \cdot \Sigma(x,t) - \Sigma(x,t)\nabla_x \log p(x,t).
  \label{eq:oracle-pressure}
\end{align}
The script \verb|scripts/check_momentum_identity.py| evaluates both sides using slow pointwise
autodifferentiation and also checks the fast batched oracle target used by the experiments. The
check reports maximum relative identity residual on the order of numerical precision and is used
as the validation for the exact-oracle appendix.

This oracle experiment does not prove that SCTW is optimal. Its role is narrower: it verifies the
mechanistic identity that motivated the material-derivative viewpoint. The practical sampler then
uses the learned model's self-curvature profile, not the oracle pressure field.
